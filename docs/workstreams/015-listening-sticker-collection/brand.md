## Common object recipe

Opaque warm ivory vinyl substrate, approximately 0.8 mm visible die-cut edge at intended scale (0.7–1 mm permitted; 3–5% of a typical sticker's short dimension). All artwork shares this edge and tiny PW / catalogue number mark. Fine distressed ink lives **under** a continuous satin laminate: printed history rather than damaged adhesive. No paper-fibre edge or individual full gloss stickers. Optional silver is a selective accent, at most 10% of face, under the same laminate; never one entire pack becoming foil. Silhouettes should remain die-cutable and recognisable around 20–35 mm. If reduced, simplify internal marks before thinning the border. Designer owns final material interpretation; its board takes precedence for exact render recipe.

Common warm ivory #F2EDDD, carbon #191919. Strong condensed / cut-and-paste lettering, micro monospaced catalogue detail, crisp silhouette, controlled speckle, one small pulse or bent signal motif linking genres. Avoid copied band marks and generic emoji sticker sets. The collection is a personal object, not a UI achievement badge system.

## Pack 01 — AFTER HOURS / metal

Mostly carbon black and bone ivory. Acid sulphur #D8EC42 may punctuate one or two designs; dull silver may cover a small detail. Dense, serrated, nocturnal. Use original gig-flyer / industrial music motifs without becoming a set of six skulls.

| Catalogue | Sticker / motif | Illustrative listening story |
|---|---|---|
| PW-A01 | **Night Shift** — moth wings fused with a speaker grille, broken halo | An evening listening session |
| PW-A02 | **Deep Cut** — excavated record shard / jagged pick with small signal line | Exploring less-played album tracks |
| PW-A03 | **Full Volume** — cracked amplifier face, angular typographic roar | Finishing a heavy album; no loudness requirement |
| PW-A04 | **No Skip** — chained circular track loop, thorned type strip | One album played front to back |
| PW-A05 | **Back Again** — coiled cable forming an irregular return loop | Returning to a favourite album |
| PW-A06 optional | **Last Light** — struck match behind a small eclipse | Listening near the end of the day |

Five core pieces above meet the pack requirement; the sixth is optional. Don't imply unsafe listening volume or reward compulsive duration. Labels belong on board captions; only short artwork words need appear on the stickers.

## Pack 02 — LOUD HEARTS / pop-rock

Hot pink #FF4D9B, cyan #27C7E8, acid lime #D8EC42, tangerine #FF863C, common carbon and ivory. Playful asymmetric cutouts, chunky lettering, racing/checker accents and CD-era shine. Keep ink wear and satin surface identical to metal.

| Catalogue | Sticker / motif | Illustrative listening story |
|---|---|---|
| PW-B01 | **On Repeat** — tangled ribbon loop, oversized pink lettering, cyan sparks | A song revisited |
| PW-B02 | **Heart Stereo** — winged heart with twin speaker cones; lime accents | Finding a favourite |
| PW-B03 | **Side Quest** — comet guitar pick dragging a checker tail | Trying an unfamiliar artist |
| PW-B04 | **Sing It Back** — open lyric burst / microphone with flower-like sound petals | Returning to a chorus favourite |
| PW-B05 | **Mixtape Kid** — original CD wallet / scribbled star and track-strip collage | Building a varied personal queue |
| PW-B06 optional | **Good Company** — two offset starbursts sharing a headphone cable | Listening across two favourite artists |

Again, five core pieces. Vary silhouette and motif rather than recolouring one badge. Shared edge/stamp/texture must be obvious when mixed with AFTER HOURS on the same iPod.

## Grounding / references

- [V&A — Lost Music Venues](https://www.vam.ac.uk/exhibitions/lost-music-venues): identifies Letraset dry-transfer Plastic People flyers by Ali Augur, 1998, and music merchandise. Supports transfer type / portable scene identity; not a source of copied artwork.
- [V&A Dundee — Ten iconic graphics from club culture history](https://www.vam.ac.uk/dundee/articles/ten-iconic-graphics-from-club-culture-history): club identity travels across tickets, stickers, sleeves, signage and flyers. Supports ephemera as a collection system.
- [Emigre — About](https://www.emigre.com/About): magazine 1984–2005 and early digital type/layout experimentation. Supports deliberately irregular digital-era lettering, not a claim that the recreated collage is an authentic Emigre artifact.
- [Emigre — Backspacer](https://www.emigre.com/Fonts/Backspacer): Nancy Mazzei and Brian Kelly, 1993. Useful typography-era anchor; no font reproduction is required.
- [UAL — UK Rave Flyers 1988/1989 interview](https://www.arts.ac.uk/study-at-ual/postgraduate-study/postgraduate-community/stories/uk-rave-flyers-19881989-a-qa-with-phatmedias-dave-nicholson): documents a collector scanning disappearing ephemera in 1998. Supports physical keepsake narrative.

The CD sheen, pop-rock candy palette and metal motif mix are our contemporary creative interpretation of the era. This board does not present AI images as historical evidence.
