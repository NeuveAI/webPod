## The contract went out first, and that was the whole point

`packages/state/src/contract.ts` was committed on its own before a single
reducer existed, because W3 is blocked on the *shape* of device state and not
on the code that moves it. Building first and publishing later would have left
that lane idle for the length of this slice.

What that cost: two later commits touch `contract.ts` again — the announcer's
settling shape, a `pageRows` field, a `BumpDirection` return, an optional
`agentOrigin`. Every one of them is additive or a narrowing that no consumer
had yet written against, and all of them landed within the same session. It is
worth naming as a hazard for anyone reading the history: **the contract commit
is a publication, not a freeze.** If W3 had already shipped against it, three
of those four edits would have needed a different treatment.

## The three things I would want a reviewer to attack first

**1. The keyboard has no acceleration, and I made the test hostile.**
The test drives 40 keydowns 5ms apart — faster than any hand and faster than
any OS auto-repeat, and far over every arc acceleration threshold — and asserts
the highlight moved exactly 40 rows with `multiplier === 1` on every single
event. If someone later "optimises" held-key scrolling, that test goes red. I
planted the optimisation myself to check: it does
(`evidence/w2-mutation-check.txt`, mutation 1).

**2. The silence rule is one line, and everything else reads it.**
`const silenced = input.source !== 'human'` inside `detent()`, and
`DetentOutcome.silenced` / `clickerTicks` / `hapticPulses` are computed from it
there. No call site re-derives it. **— Wrong, and the review caught it: the
press handler in `store.ts` had a second verbatim copy. Now `isSilenced` in
`silence.ts`, called by the reducer, the coast and the press handler. See the
review response below.** Planting `silenced = false` fails four tests
across two suites (mutation 2). The seam is built and unused: nothing in this
stage emits `"agent"` or `"system"`.

**3. The announcer is pure and the timer is not in it.**
`noteMovement` and `flushAnnouncer` take a time and return a state; the single
`setTimeout` lives in `startAnnouncer` in the store binding. That is why the
U13 gate — 30 detents, one announcement — is proved with plain integers and no
fake timers. There is also a real-timer test of the driver, so the wiring is
not taken on trust. Making every movement announce immediately fails nine tests
(mutation 3).

## What I did not build, deliberately

No React bridge hook. Keeping `packages/state` genuinely React-free is a
stronger proof of the property this slice exists for — the package has no
`react` dependency and a test asserts React is not even *resolvable* from it —
and the dispatch permits a bridge hook without requiring one.

That leaves a hazard for W3, and it is the one thing in this slice most likely
to bite someone: **pin `jotai@2.20.3` exactly.** Bun's isolated linker shares
one physical copy between workspaces asking for the same exact version, so atom
identity holds; two different versions would be two Jotai instances, and a
`Provider` from one is invisible to a hook from the other. The symptom is not a
type error, it is a component reading a default value forever. Written up in
`decisions/w2.md` §4.1.

Also not built: any tool registration, any haptics or clicker audio (the
reducer reports counts and stops there), any agent FX.

## Incident: `55b34dd` swept in six files that are not mine

Found during my own final ownership audit, after the slice was otherwise done.
Reporting it rather than quietly rewriting it.

**What happened.** `55b34dd feat(state): screen state machine` contains six
files belonging to the Apple spike lane:

```
M  docs/workstreams/002-implementation-spine/decisions/s1.md
A  docs/workstreams/002-implementation-spine/decisions/s2.md
M  docs/workstreams/002-implementation-spine/evidence/apple-capability-spike.md
A  docs/workstreams/002-implementation-spine/evidence/apple-empirical-probe.md
A  scripts/spikes/mint-apple-dev-token.ts
A  scripts/spikes/probe-apple.ts
```

**Why.** I staged my own files with an explicit pathspec and then ran a plain
`git commit -m …`. A plain commit writes **the whole index**, not the paths I
staged — so anything another lane staged into the shared index between my
`git add` and my `git commit` rode along. That is D-034's hazard, and it is not
limited to `--amend`: `git add <pathspec>` scopes the *add*, and nothing about
it scopes the *commit*.

I hit this before D-034 was published; my last three commits all use
`git commit --only -- <pathspecs>`, which builds its tree from HEAD plus the
named paths and cannot pick up a neighbour's staging. Verified after each one
that the other lane's index entries survived. **`--only` is the fix, and it
should probably be the standing rule rather than `add` + `commit`.**

**Impact: bookkeeping, not data.** Nothing was lost or altered — the six files
are committed exactly as that lane staged them, under the wrong commit message
and the wrong slice. That lane has not committed since, and still has newer
uncommitted edits to `s1.md` and `apple-capability-spike.md` sitting in the
working tree, untouched by me. Nobody needs to redo work.

**Credential check, because one of those files mints a token.** I did not open
anything under `cert/` (D-017). Checking `scripts/spikes/*.ts` at that commit
for key material by pattern count only: the single match in
`mint-apple-dev-token.ts` is `.replace(/-----BEGIN [^-]+-----/, "")`, a regex
that strips a PEM header, not embedded key material. `probe-apple.ts` matches
nothing. Neither references `cert/`, and `cert/` is in `.gitignore` and has no
tracked files. **No credential was committed.**

**I have not rewritten anything.** History rewriting is owner-only, always, and
an agent may only prepare the commands. `55b34dd` has three of my commits and
several other lanes' commits on top of it, so a clean fix is a rebase, not a
revert. My recommendation is to **leave it** — the content is correct and
present, the cost is a wrong commit message on six files, and a rebase of a
shared branch that four lanes are actively committing to is far more expensive
than the defect. If the owner would rather it be clean, the exact commands are
in `decisions/w2.md` §5, to be run by the owner and nobody else.

## Review response — REQUEST_CHANGES, four Major and five Minor

Full technical write-up in `decisions/w2.md` §6. What belongs in a diary is
what I got wrong and why I did not see it.

### The reviewer found one mistake, not nine

I enforced `silenced` and `actor` inside `detent()` so no call site could
forget them, and wrote the argument for that in three separate places. Then I
handed three other invariants I equally owned — the clock domain, the page
size, the effective density — to a consumer that does not exist yet, with no
gate and no doc. There is no principle separating the two groups. The lead
promoted it as D-049: enforce the invariants you own at the boundary you own.

I want to record *why* the split felt reasonable at the time, because the
reasoning is the part that generalises. Each of the three had a local excuse
that was individually true. The reducer is pure, so it cannot read the density
— true, but `detentActionAtom` can, and that is the seam W3 calls. The clock is
the driver's business — true, but the driver is mine too. Density reconciliation
is a rendering concern — true, and the *number of rows in a viewport* is not.
Each excuse answered a question about the module I happened to be writing at
the time rather than about the invariant. That is what to watch for.

### The one that should not have shipped

Major 1. `startAnnouncer` defaulted its clock to `Date.now()` while the due
time was built from the caller's `timestampMs`, and the idiomatic browser value
for that field is `event.timeStamp` — a different origin entirely. The reviewer
ran it: a 30-detent flick announced **30 times**. U13 did not fail, it
inverted, in the loud direction, in the exact failure mode `announce.ts`
describes in its own opening paragraph.

What stings is that I knew. The driver test writes `const start = Date.now()`
and feeds `start + i`, deliberately, because I understood the two had to agree.
I encoded that understanding in a fixture and nowhere else — not in the type,
not in a guard, not in a doc — and a fixture is a place where knowledge goes to
be invisible. The fix is not a documented rule for callers: there is now one
clock per device and no caller-supplied time reaches any comparison.

There is a Jotai footgun in that fix worth passing on to every lane:
`atom(someFunction)` does **not** make an atom holding that function. Jotai
reads a function argument as a derived atom's read callback, so the atom
computes a number and `get(clockAtom)()` throws "is not a function". Functions
have to be boxed to live in an atom.

### The one I would have argued about, and should not have

Major 2's compounding half. I had written that the reducer is pure and cannot
read the store, and treated that as settling where the page size comes from. It
settles nothing: `pageRows` was a field on the event, so any caller could pass
7 to an 8-row viewport, and the reviewer drove exactly that through the public
API. `pageRows` is gone. The page size is a parameter the store supplies. A
caller that cannot pass a wrong number is better than a caller documented not
to.

D-050 — a constant is gated only if falsifying it turns a test red — is the
most portable thing to come out of this review. My arc geometry was gated and
my density constants were not, in the same package, and I could not have told
you which was which before someone planted a value.

### Major 4, and the disclosure rule

The inertial coast was missing. That is arguable as scope. What is not arguable
is that my diary has a section headed "What I did not build, deliberately"
listing three omissions with reasons, and this fourth was not in it — three
disclosed, one not, with no principle dividing them.

I implemented it rather than adding a fourth bullet, because deferring would
have left "every coasted detent still clicks" as an invariant nobody owned,
which is the D-049 shape I was being corrected for. **What is still deferred,
and this is the disclosure:** the per-frame driver. `coastStep` is a pure
function of `(accumulator, frameSeconds)` and something has to call it about
sixty times a second. That `requestAnimationFrame` loop belongs to whoever owns
a frame loop, exactly as the announcement `setTimeout` belongs to the store —
but every rule those frames obey is in this package and tested here.

### What I did not build, deliberately — the complete list, this time

1. **A React bridge hook.** Keeping the package React-free is a stronger proof
   of the property it exists for. Now paired with D-051's singleton, which is
   the part that was actually missing.
2. **Tool registration.** No `document.modelContext`, no tools. The seam is
   `DetentSource` and the `direct` path, both unused.
3. **Clicker audio and haptics.** The reducer reports counts and stops. The
   30/sec clicker limit is a property of the speaker and belongs with it; the
   12/sec haptic suppression is here because it depends on the detent rate.
4. **The coast's frame loop.** As above. New to this list, which is the point.
   — Still true after the re-review, and still the only thing about the coast
   that is deferred. The physics it drives is now frame-rate invariant, which
   it was not when this list was written.

### On the store-outside-React claim, which was too strong

The reviewer downgraded their own endorsement of it and was right to. React
being unresolvable proves the package does not *need* React. The requirement is
that tool callbacks reach the same state the UI renders — and nothing stopped
W3 handing its `Provider` a fresh `createDeviceStore()`. Two devices, both
valid, both React-free, every test green, and the tools quietly driving a
screen nobody is looking at.

There is now one device per document, enforced two ways: `createDeviceStore`
throws outside a test runner, and a test greps `packages/` and `apps/` for
calls to it from non-test files. The static half matters more than the runtime
half — it catches the line in review, in packages this one cannot import.

### Working in the tree, second time

Every commit in this response used `git commit --only -- packages/state/src`,
and I checked `git diff --cached --name-only` before each one. Another lane had
files staged in the shared index during the first of them; they were still
staged afterwards, untouched. The mutation runs and the standalone typechecks
all happened in scratch copies, so nothing in the working tree moved.

`bun run lint` is red repo-wide right now on a `react-hooks/refs` error in the
device spike route. Not mine, and I have not touched it. `packages/state` lints
clean, which is why the gate evidence runs both and labels them.

## Re-review response — four new Majors

Technical detail in `decisions/w2.md` §8–§10. What belongs here is the pattern,
because there is one and it is not flattering.

### Three of the four were the same defect, one seam over

Round 1 taught me D-049: enforce the invariants you own at the boundary you
own. I applied it to four seams and left three open, and all three of the new
Majors are in those three.

- I fixed the announcer's two clocks and rebuilt two clocks in `BumpEvent.at`.
- I made the page size unsuppliable and left the density atoms publicly
  writable — with a comment explaining that this was fine.
- I wrote a guard for D-051 and got its arithmetic wrong in the direction that
  makes it useless.

The reviewer's diagnosis of how they found it is the part worth keeping:
**I verified the instance, they spent the principle.** My round-1 Major 1 was
"two time sources reaching one comparison is the defect, not the particular
pair" — and then I confirmed the fix by re-running the particular pair. Asking
instead "where else does a caller-supplied number and a device-clock number
land in the same field" finds `BumpEvent.at` in about a minute. Same for
density: I verified the two writers I added, rather than asking whether any
route changes density *without* reconciling.

A fix verified by re-running the failing case proves the case is fixed. It
proves nothing about the reason the case existed.

### The one that is worst

R1. `deviceStore` is built by a private path that does not increment the
counter, so in a real document the first `createDeviceStore()` succeeded — and
`<Provider store={createDeviceStore()}>`, D-051's own worked example, is
exactly one call. The guard fired on the second occurrence of a
first-occurrence problem.

And the test was green because twenty-five earlier calls in the same file had
already pushed the counter past the threshold. I wrote that test believing it
proved something. It proved that the twenty-sixth call throws.

That is D-058, and the general form is worth carrying: **a gate for a
first-occurrence invariant has to exercise the first occurrence.** The test now
runs in a fresh subprocess with a fresh module registry and no test runner, so
nothing that happened earlier in the file can make it pass — and a companion
test checks the singleton still builds there, because otherwise the first would
pass if importing the package threw.

### The one I did not expect, and the reading gap behind it

R4: the coast travelled 4× further at 30fps than at 120fps. Decay was per call,
travel was per second.

The uncomfortable part is not the arithmetic. It is that **design-system §9.4
specifies the normalisation explicitly** — *"`ω *= 0.940` at 60fps (normalised
to `ω *= 0.940^(dt/16.67ms)`)"* — and I had never read §9.4. I built the coast
from pm-spec §4.4's Release row and treated one line in a table about *input
paths* as the whole specification of a *motion* behaviour, when the motion
system has its own numbered section in the other primary source I was told to
read. The lead's ruling and the design system turned out to say the same thing;
I could have had it for free.

Fixing it properly meant three changes, not one: time-based decay, the
closed-form distance integral rather than `speed × dt`, and scoring each
coasted detent at the exact speed for its *distance* (`v = v₀ − λ·s`) rather
than at the frame's end speed. Plus stopping at the floor instead of at the
next frame boundary. The result is exact rather than within a tolerance — 16
detents, 74 rows, 240° from 15fps to 240Hz and under a jittering interval.

**And it turned up three more §9.4 / §4.4 conflicts, one of which is
significant:** the two primaries disagree on the fast-scroll curve itself —
240/540°/s → ×1/×3/×7 versus 720/1080°/s → ×1/×4/×12. I have changed nothing
and raised all three in `decisions/w2.md` §9. Picking one on my own reading
would be redesign under the cover of transcription, and it would silently
invalidate the arc-geometry gates that are currently the best-tested thing in
the package.

### On the single-commit claim

I said the four fixes were interlocked through one published contract. The
reviewer walked the symbol graph and found that only page-size × density
actually is; the coast touches no clock or density symbol and vice versa.

They were right, and the argument that landed was not about tidiness: the
+1800/−363 commit is exactly where an off-by-one counter survives review. They
named the class of defect before finding the defect in it. This round is four
commits, one per defect, each typechecking standalone with the suite green.
