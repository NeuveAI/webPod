D-060 — the coast travels the same distance at every refresh rate
==================================================================

The defect, as the re-review measured it: `coastStep` decayed velocity once per
*call* while scaling distance per *second*, so total travel was linear in the
frame interval. One identical flick:

    30fps →  32 detents, 491.9 deg
    60fps →  16 detents, 246.0 deg
    90fps →  10 detents, 164.0 deg
   120fps →   8 detents, 123.0 deg      ratio 30/120 = 4.00x

The frame COUNT to rest was 46 at every rate, which is the tell: the decay was
counting frames and the travel was counting seconds.

Why it mattered beyond correctness: this is a phone-first product, ProMotion
iPhones run at 120Hz and drop to lower rates under Low Power Mode and in
background tabs, so the same flick landed on a different row on the same device
depending on power state. 001's accessibility case is that movement is
deterministic and countable; a coast whose distance depends on the display
breaks the counting model on hardware rather than on input, which is the harm
D-042 was ruled on.

Ruling D-060: decay is a function of elapsed time, not call count.

⚑ This turned out to be a transcription gap rather than an under-specified
constant. design-system §9.4 — a primary source, which I had not read, having
worked from pm-spec §4.4 — states the normalisation outright:

    "On release, angular velocity ω decays per frame: `ω *= 0.940` at 60fps
     (normalised to `ω *= 0.940^(dt/16.67ms)`)."

Three changes, each of which the mutation file shows is load-bearing:

  1. Decay `0.94 ** (elapsed * 60)` rather than `0.94` per call — §9.4's own
     normalisation, with 60fps still seeing exactly 0.94.
  2. Distance is the closed-form integral of that decay, not `speed x dt`. A
     rectangle under an exponential is frame-rate dependent by construction.
  3. Each coasted detent is scored at the speed the wheel was doing when it
     fired, computed exactly: under `dv/dt = -λv` and `ds/dt = v`, `dv/ds = -λ`,
     so `v = v₀ - λ·s` — speed as a function of DISTANCE, which no frame rate
     can change. And the coast stops when the speed reaches the floor rather
     than at the next frame boundary.

Measured after the fix, one identical flick, every rate.

and the coast floor to 21 deg/s. The invariance is unaffected by either; the row

decays below the x4 tier quickly, so most of the glide is at x1.
