## W4-D1 · The §12.3 material table lives in `packages/device`, not `packages/tokens`

**The conflict.** D-012 says W4's material parameters default to "the §12.3
values **from `packages/tokens`**". The W4 guardrail says W4 owns
`packages/device/**` and `[_]spike.` routes **only**.

**Decision.** The guardrail wins. `packages/device/src/materials.ts` is the one
place a material number is written, shaped so it can be lifted into
`packages/tokens` verbatim by the lane that owns that package.

**What is *not* affected.** Geometry. §12.0's radii are already exported and
test-locked by W0, and `layout.ts` **imports** every one of them — `WHEEL_R`,
`BODY_W/H`, `SELECT_R`, `SELECT_LIP_R`, `LABEL_BAND_INNER_R/OUTER_R`,
`RECESS_SHADOW_REACH_R`, `PANEL_W/H`, `PANEL_SCALE`, `BODY_CORNER_R`. Nothing in
`packages/device` re-types a §12.0 number.

**How to falsify me:** if the lead holds that D-012's wording is the binding
half, the fix is to move one file and re-export it, and no call site changes.

## W4-D2 · The steel is a `MeshPhysicalMaterial`, not a `MeshStandardMaterial`

§12.3's steel row says `MeshStandardMaterial` **and** `anisotropy 0.75`.
three.js has no `anisotropy` on `MeshStandardMaterial` — it is declared on
`MeshPhysicalMaterial` (`src/materials/MeshPhysicalMaterial.js:60-90`, and
grepping `MeshStandardMaterial.js` for it returns nothing). The row cannot be
implemented as written.

**Decision.** Use `MeshPhysicalMaterial`, which *extends* `MeshStandardMaterial`,
so every other parameter in the row is honoured and the anisotropy is too.

**The alternative and why not.** Dropping the anisotropy would keep the class
name and delete §10.4 prevention #5 — "anisotropic single-direction grain at
≤5% … is what stops the surface reading as chrome-plated plastic". Between a
class name and an anti-slop guard, the guard is the one that matters.

## W4-D3 · `flat` — tone mapping off — is a correctness requirement, not a look

R3F's default is `ACESFilmicToneMapping` (`docs/API/canvas.mdx:44`), a filmic
S-curve that rolls highlights off and maps linear 1.0 to roughly 0.8 display.
§4.4's `--steel-0` is `#F6F8FA`, 250 units. Under ACES that value is
**arithmetically unreachable** however bright the room gets, and the same is
true at the top of §4.3 and §4.5's white variants.

**Decision.** `flat` on the `<Canvas>`, i.e. `NoToneMapping`. §12.3's stop
tables are stated as sRGB *output* values, so the output transform has to be the
plain sRGB encode.

## W4-D4 · The click wheel ring is **dished**, and §5.3 L4's "domed" is superseded

§4.5's ring table runs `#14161A` at the top to `#2C3037` at the bottom and
attributes the dark top to "shadow from the recess lip". That attribution does
not survive the move to 3D: with the key at 18° off vertical (LAW 2), a lip
standing `h` above the ring casts a shadow reaching `0.32·h` down the face, and
§5.3 puts the ring **1.5px** below the body plane. That buys half a pixel of
shadow for a ramp spread over ~88px. Casting it geometrically would need a pit
about 270px deep in a device 59px thick.

**Decision.** The ring is a shallow concave dish. On a bowl lit from 12 o'clock
the upper wall's normal leans away from the key and the lower wall's leans into
it; at a ~10° rim tilt the `N·L` ratio bottom-to-top is ≈3.4, against the 3.5
the §4.5 table asks for. The specified appearance and the physical object agree
— only §4.5's *explanation* was 2D.

**The conflict this creates, stated rather than absorbed.** §5.3 L4 calls the
wheel face "very slightly **domed**" with a sheen at upper-left, which is the
opposite curvature. The token table wins, because it is the acceptance criterion
and L4's sheen is a 9%-white overlay. The Select plug keeps §4.5's stated
geometry and stays **convex** — "the only raised element on the wheel" — and the
inversion between the two is what makes it pop.

## W4-D5 · §4.2 and §4.4 are not simultaneously satisfiable by one room — D-057

**The arithmetic.** At the row where a face's normal points at the camera, both
the polycarbonate front and the steel back reflect the *same* direction, so the
room's radiance there is pinned by whichever table is env-driven.

- Steel: `metalness 1`, `color #C4CBD2` → `F0` ≈ 0.591 (luma-weighted linear).
  `--steel-5 #7C858F` is 0.232 linear, so the room must emit **0.393**.
- Black poly: `reflectivity 0.55` → `ior` 1.564 → base `F0` 0.0484, plus
  `clearcoat 1.0` at `F0` 0.04. Total specular response ≈ **0.09**.
- 0.393 × 0.09 = 0.0354 linear = **≈50 units**. §4.2's dead zone is `#0C0D0F`,
  **12.9 units**. The two are a factor of ~11 apart and no light rig closes it,
  because neither surface can be given a different reflection direction from the
  other at that row.

**Decision, ratified by the lead as D-057.** `envMapIntensity` — which §12.3
does not state — differs per surface: the steel stays at **1.0** so the room is
still exactly what the mirror reads as §4.4, and the front surfaces sit low so
they are lit rather than reflective.

**The reading that makes this the right answer rather than a fudge**, and the
general form the lead asked to be recorded here:

> ⚑ **§4.2 appears to be a 2D paint spec — the pixel values a designer wants —
> rather than a measurement of a surface in the same room as §4.4. Both are
> legitimate and they are not interchangeable. When a stop table and a material
> recipe disagree, ask which is describing light and which is describing paint
> before assuming either is wrong.**

001 describes the two differently and consistently with that: §4.2's stops are
*light* — "reflected key light through the plastic's surface layer", "floor
bounce begins", "bottom edge caustic" — while §4.4's are *reflections* —
"ceiling reflection", "upper-wall specular band", "the room's dark half". Poly
light-driven, steel env-driven. Giving them the same room radiance was 001's
implicit error, not a defect in either table.

**What this does not license.** Painting a gradient onto a material. The steel's
non-monotonic horizon band is still a reflection of a room generated from the
§4.4 table plus §5.2's sky blob and horizon line, and it still moves when the
camera moves — which is the test that it is a reflection and not a fill.

## W4-D6 · The equirect texel→direction mapping is inverted from the obvious one

**The bug.** I generated the room by walking image rows from the top, writing the
zenith into row 0 the way one writes an image. three samples an equirect through
`equirectUv` (`common.glsl.js:95-97`), where `v = asin(dir.y)/π + 0.5` — so
`v = 1` is the zenith — and a `DataTexture` has `flipY = false`, which puts data
row 0 at `v = 0`, the **nadir**. The room was upside down.

**Why it is worth an entry.** It does not throw, and on a diffuse surface it does
not look wrong. On the mirror it produced a perfectly plausible smooth gradient
with §4.4's profile reversed, which reads as "the rig needs tuning" rather than
"the room is inverted" — and a rig tuner will spend a long time failing to fix it
from the outside. The fix is to invert `equirectUv` exactly rather than to write
an image.

**Standing lesson:** when a generated texture feeds a renderer's sampling
convention, derive the generator from the sampling function in the renderer's
source. Do not infer it from what the texture looks like in an image viewer.

**Its twin, found the same way.** §4.4's gradient is `168deg`, and CSS angles are
clockwise from "to top" in a **y-down** space. In `luminance-probe.ts`'s y-**up**
frame the 0%→100% direction is `(sin θ, +cos θ)`, not `(sin θ, −cos θ)`. The sign
error graded the plate against §4.4 read bottom-to-top and looked, again, exactly
like a rig that would not converge.

## W4-D7 · The env map ships at 2048 × 1024, not §12.3's 512 × 256

§4.4's end stops are fast: `--steel-0` → `--steel-1` falls 44 units across 7% of
the plate and `--steel-9` → `--steel-10` falls 76 across 6%. At the framing
distance, 6% of the plate is under two degrees of reflected elevation — three
texels at 256 rows, before `PMREMGenerator` prefilters on top. Measured, those
two stops came back damped by 12 and 16 units and no rig parameter recovered
them; raising the room's resolution did, directly.

**Decision.** 2048 × 1024. §12.3's number is a floor for the construction, not a
ceiling on the sampling rate, and the acceptance criterion is the stop table.

**Cost, stated.** RGBA float at that size is 32MB, held for the life of the
material. It is generated once and never re-uploaded, so it does not touch
§14.1's budget — which is about rAF callbacks, not bytes — but it is the largest
single allocation in the package and the obvious first thing to halve
(`HalfFloatType`) when a memory budget appears.

## W4-D8 · The chassis is three meshes, because a recess cannot live inside a slab

The obvious model is one steel slab with a polycarbonate plate on the front. It
is wrong, and it failed silently: the click wheel is a **recess**, so the ring
surface sits several pixels *behind* the body face — inside the slab. The first
render of this scene sampled the wheel at 210 units against §4.5's 22, because
what the probe was reading through the recess opening was the steel's own front
cap, tinted `#C4CBD2` and looking entirely plausible.

**Decision.** `steelShell` (perimeter frame and opening walls, cut by the same
two holes as the plastic), `steelBackPlate` (§5.2's mirror, uncut), and
`frontPlate` (§5.1/§4.3, inset by the seam). The seam of §5.6 is then not a
stroke at all — it is the steel shell's own rolled edge showing past the inset
plastic, which gives §10.4 prevention #6's conic response for free because a
rolled edge presents a different angle to the light at every point of a rounded
rectangle.

## W4-D9 · The spike route's tunables are a module store read through `useSyncExternalStore`

**The tension.** Repo law bans `useState`. The rig tuner has to move ~25 scalars
from outside React, over CDP, and see the scene re-render.

**Decision.** A module-level object plus `useSyncExternalStore`. Not `useState`
— which the tuner could not reach at all — and not Jotai, which is not a
dependency of `packages/device` or `apps/web` and belongs to W2. The law's
stated purpose is that *callbacks outside React read and write the same state
the UI renders*, and this is precisely that shape.

⚑ The probe reads the store directly rather than through a ref mirroring a
prop. Writing `ref.current = props.x` during render is what `react-hooks/refs`
forbids, and the ref would have been a second copy of a value that already lives
outside React.

**D-055 has no surface here.** There are no Jotai atoms in `packages/device` at
all, so there is nothing to box.

## W4-D10 · The luminance targets are stated, not eyeballed — and one of them is a pair

A §4.2 stop is a **row**, not a point: any pixel on that row that is on the right
surface carries the specified value. That matters because the obvious column —
straight down the middle — is behind the glass or the wheel for two thirds of
the body's height. Each target therefore names its row exactly and takes the
outermost exposed column on it.

⚑ **Body, ring and Select targets are sampled as a mirrored `±x` pair and
averaged.** The render is deliberately not laterally symmetric — LAW 2's fill
comes from the lower-left and §5.2 L3's blob sits upper-left — so a single
column reads one side of an asymmetry the spec *asks for* and grades it as
error. Averaging is what makes the reading a **vertical** sample of a gradient
defined as a function of `y` alone.

The steel is different and is handled differently: at `168deg` a stop is a line
**perpendicular to the gradient axis**, and the 0% and 100% lines pass through
opposite *corners* of the box. Each steel stop is therefore read at the midpoint
of its iso-line's chord across the plate, clamped into the silhouette — sampling
`--steel-0` down the middle would read whatever sits at 3.7% instead.

## W4-D11 · Select-button alpha is dropped from the targets, deliberately

§4.5's `--select-k-*` / `--select-w-*` carry 88–96% alpha over the ring beneath.
Compositing the stated alpha over the stated ring value moves each target by
under one unit — `#1B1E23` at 90% over `#23262B` reads 27.8/30.8/35.8 against
27/30/35 — so the alpha is dropped and this is the justification rather than an
oversight.

## W4-D12 · Not built, and why

- **§5.7 engraved Apple logo / wordmark on the steel.** §12.3 recipe 7 wants a
  normal map. It carries no information (§4.4's own note: WCAG 1.4.3 exempts
  logotypes), it is outside every stop table, and it is on the face the expose
  flip reveals — which is W6's, not built yet. Deferred deliberately, not missed.
- **§5.5 L12–13 chromatic edge refraction** (§12.3 recipe 3, a custom shader) and
  **§5.2 L6's anisotropy *map*** (recipe 2, a tangent-space texture). The
  material's scalar `anisotropy 0.75` is applied; the 1024² map is not. Both are
  shader/texture work with no stop-table consequence, and the packet's rule is
  one implementation and no speculative variants.
- **Accelerometer shimmer.** §14.1 permits it under strict conditions. It is not
  built, so there is no `deviceorientation` listener, no permission prompt and
  no 20Hz sampler — which is also the cleanest way to satisfy "never requested
  on load". The static rig is the §14.1 fallback and it is not a flat surface.

## W4-D13 · The far-light solve does not repeal D-057

The closed-form solve found that the black and white **front** stop tables can
share LAW 2's key and fill when the lights are far enough away to behave almost
directionally. That invalidates the earlier close-light search space, not
D-057. The steel back still receives most of its appearance from the room map,
while the fronts receive most of theirs from the two lights. Moving the lights
farther away does not change the room radiance reflected by a face normal, so
the front-vs-steel incompatibility derived in W4-D5 remains.

## W4-D14 · Wheel legends are a transparent decal, not an albedo division

The inherited implementation divided the desired legend colour by the dark
wheel albedo and clamped the result. Every channel therefore became white and,
after multiplication by the wheel material, the legend became the wheel colour:
mathematically invisible. The legends now live on a separate transparent,
non-tone-mapped decal 0.08 body units above the dish. The physical ring remains
one material and the decal carries only ink, matching the distinct layers in
Pencil nodes `A76Ib`, `DLqSo`, `H4QpB` and `VWaJS`.

## W4-D15 · The glass uses a temporary alpha boundary around three transmission

`src/materials/MeshPhysicalMaterial.js` lines 519–525 in the three.js 0.185.0
clone says that when `transmission` is non-zero, `opacity` should be 1. In this
scene the stock transmission path sampled its own off-screen transmission pass
as an opaque grey rectangle over the separately owned screen mesh. That broke
D-056 visually even though the boundary type remained correct.

The cover retains the specified `transmission 0.92`, `ior 1.52`, thickness and
clearcoat, but is also alpha-bounded at 0.12 so the screen is visible in the W4
preview. This is an explicit temporary deviation, not a claim about three's
recommended material setup. W6 may replace only the cover material with a
screen-aware shader; the `ScreenMeshHandle` and screen geometry stay unchanged.

## W4-D16 · The visible perimeter is a restrained chrome seam

The inherited shell used the mirror-back material on the narrow front perimeter,
turning a seam into a neon-white outline. The seam now has its own chrome
material (`#98A1AA`, metalness 1, roughness 0.18, low room intensity), while the
back plate retains the high-contrast mirror recipe. This preserves the physical
three-mesh chassis from W4-D8 without collapsing two visually different roles.

## W4-D17 · Screen UVs belong to the D-056 producer

The first composite preview exposed a producer-side seam defect:
`ExtrudeGeometry` generated cap UVs in raw shape coordinates, so the 272 × 204
screen supplied values far outside `[0,1]`. `HTMLTexture` correctly clamped
those values, producing repeated horizontal edge bands instead of a panel.

The fix is device-owned. `createScreenGeometry()` rewrites every UV from body
coordinates to normalized panel coordinates, with conventional texture
orientation: top-left `(0,1)`, top-right `(1,1)`, bottom-right `(1,0)`,
bottom-left `(0,0)`. The seam test checks every vertex against that transform
and checks all four extrema. Composite and panel code were not changed.

## W4-D18 · Holdout material identity outranks an overfit stop profile

The initial calibration exposed 111 scalar controls, including independent
vertical and lateral normal tilts at the exact grading rows. That can encode the
answer key. Its best checkpoint passed 32/43, but independent light/dark
captures showed horizontal blue bands, a bronze black shell, and merged white
surfaces. Pencil VWaJS instead has a smooth pearl body, and the owner explicitly
rejected those bands.

Product defaults and the active tuner therefore use flat optical profiles. The
checked JSON preserves the regularized result (8/43) rather than presenting a
visually invalid fit as convergence. The conflict is bounded: the black table
asks one smooth face to move 64.8 → 12.9 → 53.6 luminance, and white asks 255 →
222.5 → 253.8, while the binding Pencil frame has no corresponding row-local
bands. Resolution requires a revised sampling contract or a new physically
justified macro-geometry/light model—not more per-stop knobs.

## W4-D19 · VWaJS enclosure outline and unclipped framing

VWaJS measures 330 × 552 with a visibly circular 26 px corner. The older 4.2
superellipse reads too square at preview scale, and distance 980 made it worse
by projecting the body outside its 330 × 552 canvas. The rendered enclosure now
uses the observed 26 px circular outline and distance 1160. Internal screen and
wheel geometry remains locked. This is recorded visual-source correction, not
a silent token-table change.

## W4-D20 · D-067 makes geometry and probe identity one contract

D-067 supersedes the unresolved-source wording in W4-D19. Saved Pencil VWaJS
is authoritative: 330×552 with a circular 26px corner. `BODY_CORNER_R` and
`BODY_CORNER_EXPONENT` are the only definition; `DEVICE_LAYOUT`, renderer,
shadow, probe and tests consume them.

Probe positions alone are not evidence. Before a sampled framebuffer pixel is
graded, a raycast must hit the target's expected named mesh and material. Body
and steel targets keep a 3px interior margin; recessed wheel and Select targets
keep 6px. A mismatch throws and the tuner rejects the candidate. The former
8/43 and 32/43 reports are invalid because they had neither shared geometry nor
identity admission.

The final candidate intentionally rejects a later 28/32 front fit whose
stop-local normals visibly faceted both colourways. It retains smooth profiles,
restores the exact black sheen 0.15 / `#6E4A2E`, corrects the lower-edge SSS
orientation, and uses VWaJS's thin cool seam. Its checked result is 6/43, RMS
16.36, worst 45.42; this does not clear the ±4 gate or owner-only H-6/U14.

## W4-D21 · Lighting and steel stops are defined in the viewer frame

LAW 2's key and fill are viewer/world-space lights, not children bolted to the
iPod. They are siblings of `device-model`; the front/back switch rotates only
that model. Probe points are body-local inputs to the live model matrix.

The steel table's 168° direction is also viewer-facing. Its target solver first
finds the point in that frame, then negates x before storing the back-face local
point because the π Y rotation mirrors x. Reconstructing the gradient parameter
after `localToWorld` is the gate. Without the inverse transform, four off-axis
stops move to 0.109/0.117/0.883/0.891 instead of their intended iso-lines.

## W4-D22 · Calibration admits the first visible surface or fails closed

Raycaster results are distance-sorted. Calibration consumes that order and does
not search for a later expected name. Object/material invisibility follows the
renderer; the only alpha-aware exception is `wheel-label-decal` when its
generated `CanvasTexture` pixel is readable and exactly transparent. Every
unknown, unreadable, unnamed, or wrong-material first hit rejects the sample.

The live guard exposed a real overlapping cap in the steel shell. The shell is
now an analytic perimeter frame with a central hole, rather than relying on
depth ordering to show polycarbonate behind steel. Three's constructor-set
`isMesh`, `isMaterial`, `isMeshBasicMaterial`, and `isCanvasTexture` flags are
used at the workspace package boundary because `instanceof` is not stable
across Vite's duplicated module graph.

## W4-D23 · Gate order and the final bounded smooth candidate

The calibrator's objective is lexicographic: fewer failed ±4 rows, then smaller
worst overage, then lower MSE. Invalid geometry outranks nothing and can never
be accepted as a one-row result. Room/front search modes change only which of
the existing authorized knobs are scored; they do not add product degrees.

The one macro-geometry addition is a quadratic vertical crown over the complete
front plate, zero at its top/bottom join and controlled by one scalar. The final
retained value is −2.125 body px; all stop-local optical profiles remain flat.
After the required final front-only run the valid report is **24/43**, RMS
**9.47**, worst **19.99**, steel **10/11**. This is the final red result for
re-review, not a waiver or a claim that the sources conflict. H-6 and U14 remain
owner-only.

## W4-D24 · Calibration claims require an immutable source identity

The reviewed baseline is commit `3c5db2a`, not a live rig or a temporary seed.
The rig, package defaults, optical profiles, material scopes, applicator and
tests move together. Evidence records commit/tree, rig and lock digests,
archive file count, browser build and report digest. A live working-tree result
cannot support a conflict claim.

## W4-D25 · Uniform sheen roughness is live and falsified

retained as a tuned deviation.

## W4-D27 · White hierarchy uses depth and a restrained seam

Pencil VWaJS shows a pale wheel recessed from a pearl shell and a thin cool
blue-gray boundary. The wheel recess is restored to the stated 1.5px and the
seam is now a restrained #A6AFBA, rough 0.32, partially metallic boundary

## W4-D28 · Pencil supersedes the legacy stop table for final W4 appearance

The owner rejected the calibrated gray/flat render and named saved Pencil

with the mandated physical model and approved a visual-authority ruling. This
