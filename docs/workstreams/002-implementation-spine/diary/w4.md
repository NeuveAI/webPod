to touch this will meet at least one of them again.

## The shape of the work, decided early

The gate is a **measurement**, so the first real decision was to build the
measurement before the object: `luminance-probe.ts` states where every §4.2–4.5
stop lives on the device and what it should read, with no renderer in scope, and
`scripts/w4-tune.ts` drives the running route over CDP and moves the rig until
the numbers land. Everything after that was measured rather than eyeballed. It
also meant every bug below announced itself as a number instead of as a feeling.

## Three silent failures, in the order they bit

**1. A self-intersecting silhouette.** The superellipse outline is built as four
corner arcs, and half of them have to be reversed *and* the four visited in the
order the outline walks. I got the second part wrong. earcut triangulated the
resulting bowtie into four detached corner brackets, and what rendered was the
steel behind the plastic — which reads as a material bug, not a winding one.

**2. The recess inside the slab.** The obvious chassis is one steel slab with a
plastic plate on the front. The click wheel is a *recess*, so the ring sits
behind the body face — inside the slab. The wheel sampled at **210 units against
§4.5's 22**, and the reason was that the probe was reading the steel's own front
cap through the opening. Tinted `#C4CBD2`, perfectly plausible, completely
wrong. Fixed by cutting the openings through the steel too, which is W4-D8's
three-mesh chassis and which incidentally gives §5.6's seam as real geometry.

**3. The room upside down, twice.** three samples an equirect with
`v = asin(dir.y)/π + 0.5`, so `v = 1` is the zenith — and a `DataTexture` has
`flipY = false`, so data row 0 is the **nadir**. I wrote the sky into row 0 the
way one writes an image. Then, having fixed that, I found the same class of
error on the other side of the measurement: §4.4's `168deg` is a CSS angle,
clockwise from "to top" in a **y-down** space, and the probe's frame is y-up, so
the gradient axis needed `+cos θ` where I had `−cos θ`.

⚑ **Both of these produced a smooth, plausible metal with §4.4's profile
reversed.** Neither threw. Both look exactly like "the rig needs tuning", and I
spent a tuning run on each before reading the shader source. The lesson is in
`decisions/w4.md` W4-D6 and it is the one I would most want carried forward:
when a generated texture feeds a renderer's sampling convention, derive the
generator from the sampling function **in the renderer's source**, not from what
the texture looks like in an image viewer.

## The finding that needed a ruling

Partway through tuning it became clear the rig was being asked to do something
impossible, so I did the arithmetic rather than keep searching. At the row where
a face's normal points at the camera, the polycarbonate front and the steel back
reflect the *same* direction — so the room's radiance there is pinned by
whichever table is env-driven, and §4.4 pins it at 0.393 linear while §4.2's
dead zone needs the poly to read 12.9 units, which at the poly's ≈0.09 specular
response means the room must be at 0.035. A factor of eleven, and no light rig
removes it.

Raised with the full derivation rather than absorbed. The lead ratified the
resolution as **D-057** and sharpened the reasoning: 001 *describes* the two
tables differently — §4.2's stops are light ("reflected key light", "floor
bounce", "bottom edge caustic"), §4.4's are reflections ("ceiling reflection",
"the room's dark half") — so poly is light-driven and steel is env-driven, and
giving them one room radiance was 001's implicit error. The general form is
recorded in `decisions/w4.md` W4-D5.

## The boundary

Proposed to the lead ahead of the rest of the work, since W6 was blocked on it,
and approved unchanged as **D-056**. Applying **D-054** meant removing three
slots where a stale transform could exist rather than documenting three rules:
no `Object3D` on the handle at all (there is nowhere to read an un-updated
`matrixWorld` from), the change signal *carries* the `ScreenTransform` rather
than handing back the handle, and the viewport projection is done device-side so
W6 cannot get the y-flip or the DPR wrong with no error. The change signal hangs
off `onBeforeRender` rather than `useFrame`, so there is no `useFrame` anywhere
in `packages/device` and an idle device costs nothing at all.

The lead attached two conditions: no raw `Object3D` for now, and the corner
convention pinned by a test on **both** sides of the seam. `screen-mesh.test.ts`
is my half; W6 owns the other.

## Tuning, and what it cost

The rig tuner is coordinate descent over ~25 scalars, in two stages, because the
two halves of the object are driven by different things (D-057) — scoring all
forty-three stops together let the search trade the eleven steel stops away for
the thirty-two front ones, which it did, parking a bright blob nine degrees wide
directly on the steel's sampled column. Stage one fixes the room against the
steel alone; stage two freezes it and lights the front.

Two corrections the measurements forced, both in `decisions/w4.md`:

- **W4-D7** — the room ships at 2048 × 1024, not §12.3's 512 × 256. §4.4's end
  stops fall 44 and 76 units across 6–7% of the plate, which at the framing
  distance is under two degrees of reflected elevation. At 256 rows they came
  back damped by 12 and 16 units and no rig parameter recovered them.
- The residual after that was **antisymmetric and local** — adjacent stops off in
  opposite directions wherever the table turns fast — which is a blur signature,
  not a gain error. A global contrast control could not fix it and made the slow
  middle worse. An unsharp mask along the elevation profile is the correct
  inverse and it is what closed most of the gap.

## What I did not build, deliberately

§5.7's engraved logo (a normal map, on the face W6's flip reveals, carrying no
information), §5.5's chromatic edge refraction and §5.2's anisotropy *map* (both
shader/texture work with no stop-table consequence), and the accelerometer
shimmer. The last is worth naming: not building it is also the cleanest way to
satisfy §14.1's "never requested on load" — there is no `deviceorientation`
listener and no permission prompt anywhere in this package. The static LAW 2 rig
is the fallback §14.1 asks for, and it is not a flat surface.

## Replacement pass after the first owner preview

The first owner preview failed H-6 decisively: silver-grey slab, collapsed
material separation, broad striped reflections, a neon perimeter, an opaque
raised grey screen, and no wheel legends. I preserved the existing geometry,
tuner and D-056 boundary and corrected those failure modes in place.

Pencil was re-read through MCP at `A76Ib`, `DLqSo`, `H4QpB` and `VWaJS`. The
black references establish neutral glossy polycarbonate, a genuinely recessed
dark display well, a distinct wheel and Select plug, and visible MENU/transport
legends; the white references establish the same separations without making the
body a grey metal plate. The replacement render now has those layers, including
a separate legend decal and a restrained chrome seam.

The far-light solve was also reconciled explicitly with D-057. It improves the
shared front-light problem; it does not change the room reflection that made the
front and steel-back recipes incompatible at one env-map intensity.

The final resumed tuning pass improved the quantitative result without changing
the material model: **15/43** samples pass, RMS error **10.13**, worst error
**25.19**. Steel remains close
(9/11 pass, worst 5.08); the front's non-monotonic profiles remain outside ±4.
The preview is materially corrected, but H-6 remains owner-only and uncleared.

## Composite seam correction

W6's first HTMLTexture integration found that the screen's ExtrudeGeometry UVs
were raw body coordinates rather than normalized texture coordinates. This was
not a composite defect: the consumer received a quad whose UV contract was
undefined and three clamped it into horizontal edge bands. The device now owns
`createScreenGeometry()`, normalizes every vertex, and tests the full `[0,1]`
range plus TL/TR/BR/BL orientation. The flagged Chrome 151 composite route was
rerun after the fix and rendered the complete menu texture. No composite or
panel file was edited.

## Final visual regularization and bounded escalation

The 43-point optimizer found a misleading local optimum: 32/43 stops passed,
but only after assigning independent vertical and lateral normal tilts to every
sample row. Holdouts showed what the scalar score hid—broad blue and bronze
stripes across the body and wheel. This failed Pencil and the owner's material
identity check, so those profiles were removed from product defaults and from
the tuner's search space. Wheel and Select depth now comes from geometry and
materials.

The square silhouette had a separate cause. At 30° FOV and distance 980, the
552 px body projects beyond its same-sized canvas; the rounded corners were
clipped away. Distance 1160 exposes the full outline. The rendered shell now
follows VWaJS's observed circular 26 px corner while screen and wheel layout
remain unchanged.

The regularized render is the final visual candidate: white reads pearl/cool
silver with a legible panel and separated wheel; black remains visible in the
dark room without fitted bands. The pointwise report is intentionally red
(8/43) rather than shipping the visually false 32/43 fit. This is escalated as
a source conflict; H-6 remains owner-only.

## D-067 correction and final handoff

D-067 resolved the enclosure conflict in favour of saved Pencil component
VWaJS: 330×552, circular radius 26. Radius and exponent now originate together
in `@webpod/tokens`; layout, all three enclosure meshes, the CSS shadow, probe,
and tests consume that definition. The old renderer-local 26/2 pair is gone.

The probe now performs a raycast before admitting a framebuffer pixel. Object
and material names must match the target, with a 3px body/back inset and 6px
recessed-control inset. This invalidates both the old 8/43 and 32/43 reports:
they used stale geometry and could count seam/room pixels as body endpoints.

One last optimizer run reached 28/32 front stops only by restoring large
stop-local normal changes. Its holdout showed faceted blue/bronze glare and a
merged white face, so it was rejected rather than checked in. The retained
candidate has flat optical profiles, a Pencil-scale 2px cool chrome seam, the
exact black BRDF (`sheen 0.15`, `#6E4A2E`), and corrected lower-edge SSS map
orientation. The final strict report is **6/43**, RMS **16.36**, worst **45.42**.
That is red evidence, not acceptance. H-6 and U14 remain owner-only.
