# Independent review — replacement for rejected Riff Ritual

**Final assessment: both illustrated proposals are ready to show as alternatives; neither is adopted or user-approved.** Heavy Rotation is my stronger recommendation for its integrated record/chain composition and clearer repeated-listening idea. Riff Ritual is a viable, denser instrument-led alternative. The user rejected Wm3Tb, and my earlier round-two pass is withdrawn: that review over-weighted palette/legibility and failed to judge whether the artwork was worth presenting.

## Evidence and failure diagnosis

Read riff-replacement.md and inspected the exact user rejection screenshot on 2026-09-05. The large black silhouette has arbitrary notches and no recognisable musical object. Plain rectangular condensed type is pasted into it. A disconnected jack and three tiny scratches do not create an illustrated idea. The negative space is leftover acreage rather than intentional composition. Surface wear looks token. Calling that a simplified collection direction was an inadequate standard.

Successful Night Shift and No Skip from prior independently inspected collection screenshots show why this matters: the moth/speaker or chain-loop physically defines the silhouette; lettering sits within an integrated nameplate/banner; cream details describe the subject and create hierarchy. They can be recognised before their words are read. Matching a colour value alone does not recreate that craft.

## Visual gates for both alternatives

- One evocative, recognisable music illustration per sticker. Heavy Rotation and Riff Ritual must offer meaningfully different subjects/compositions, not a palette/title swap.
- Lettering and subject physically compose together: wrap, overlap, banner, negative-space fit or other purposeful relationship. No plain title dropped over a generic shape.
- Outer cutline follows an intentional illustrated silhouette. No arbitrary blob, accidental protrusions or detached garnish.
- Cream and carbon create visual hierarchy and readable negative spaces. Detailed worn print contributes to form, not random scratched overlays.
- Full-size inspect joins, overlaps, plausible instrument/record/chain geometry and typography. No malformed letters, ambiguous object fusions or untraceable decorative cables.
- At approximately 80–100 px wide, title and primary motif survive; micro details may recede. Empty black mass must not swallow subject identity.
- Pair visibly belongs near Night Shift and No Skip through ivory edge, print density, expressive music-ephemera character and comparable illustration ambition. Do not copy their subjects.
- Presentation must honestly label two proposals. No pack/device replacement until user chooses.

## Full-size candidate assessment

Inspected actual generated candidates and final alpha images `docs/design/images/playworn-heavy-rotation-proposal-v1-alpha.png` and `playworn-riff-ritual-proposal-v1-alpha.png`.

Heavy Rotation is the stronger visual concept: expressive HEAVY lettering, elliptical vinyl grooves, encircling chain and ROTATION banner interlock rather than sit as unrelated symbols. Directional grooves and chain orbit communicate repetition/rotation. Cream highlights describe form throughout, with deliberate dark-light hierarchy. The cutline follows the illustration and letter shapes.

Riff Ritual offers a meaningfully different illustrated instrument scene: aggressive title, guitar headstock, amplifier and curved banner have physical overlap and coordinated diagonals. The grille and worn ink give appropriate music-ephemera density. Cable paths are partially hidden by the amp/banner; no exposed fork is obvious, though the perimeter is busier and less immediately simple than Heavy Rotation. This is a legitimate richer alternative, unlike the rejected sparse assembly.

Both materially exceed the rejected artwork in integrated composition, illustration effort and evocative character. Final alpha images retain these qualities. The actual 90px proofs were then inspected as described below.
